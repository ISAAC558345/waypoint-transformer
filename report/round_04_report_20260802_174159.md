## Data construction and shapes

The target is the raw physical displacement:

```text
delta_xy[t,H] = q[t+H] - q[t]
normalized_delta_xy = delta_xy / (H * max_speed * delta_t)
```

The model predicts exactly two coordinates, `[delta_x, delta_y]`. Radial
projection to the reachable unit disk is evaluation-only and is never part of
the model or loss.

## Leakage audit

The pre-training audit is
`report/round_04_input_leakage_audit_20260802_1558.md`.

- Current state features are causal and contain no undeclared future target.
- `achieved_terminal_goal` is explicitly declared hindsight information and
  is kept in a separate goal channel, never folded into state.
- The state-only ablation removes all 13 goal coordinates while keeping the
  same target, split, architecture family, optimizer, and seeds.
- Target endpoint, future displacement, terminal metadata, policy label,
  quality label, episode length, and grouping fields cannot enter predictors.
- There are 400 terminal-endpoint samples for which the hindsight terminal
  goal can directly reveal the endpoint. This is an acknowledged shortcut
  risk, not hidden leakage.
- The desired-goal channel has only two effective patterns in the pilot, so it
  was retained as an audit comparison rather than used as the primary goal.

Consequently there is no undeclared leakage, but the achieved-terminal goal
must not be described as a deployment-time causal goal.

## Model and training protocol

The model is a direct relative-displacement MLP with three 256-unit ReLU
hidden layers, no dropout, and an identity two-dimensional output. Its loss is
exactly:

```text
mean(sum((prediction_normalized - target_normalized) ** 2, axis=coordinate))
```

Training used Adam, learning rate `1e-3`, batch size 256, weight decay 0,
gradient clipping 1, maximum 300 epochs, patience 30, deterministic PyTorch,
and seeds 0/1/2. Best checkpoints use minimum validation future-endpoint RMSE.
Test tensors are absent from the trainer interface and are evaluated only
after the horizon ranking is immutable.

The official repository's generic goal-network topology and initialization
semantics were retained. A plain PyTorch trainer was added because the
original high-level trainer is coupled to D4RL/Gym/W&B/legacy Lightning task
modules and cannot consume the locked maritime NPZ schema without importing
unrelated environments.

Every run wrote `best.pt`, `last.pt`, resolved config, training history, and
result JSON. Checkpoints bind source hashes, implementation/config hashes,
train/validation tensor hashes, model kind, H, dimensions, RNG state, and
optimizer state. Resume rejects any provenance mismatch.

## Validation-only horizon selection

The declared composite score is:

```text
0.55 * mean normalized endpoint error
+ 0.10 * endpoint RMSE / largest candidate validation endpoint RMSE
+ 0.15 * seed std(normalized endpoint error)
+ 0.10 * (1 - direction cosine)
+ 0.10 * (1 - reachable rate)
```

Raw displacement RMSE and endpoint RMSE are mathematically identical for a
relative target and are recorded separately but included only once in the
score.

H=30 is therefore the locked recommendation. H=20 later has slightly lower

horizons are intrinsically easier.

## Policy, buoy-count, quality, and length groups

Selected-H test results expose strong heterogeneity:

| Collection policy | Goal RMSE [m] | Zero RMSE [m] | Goal vs zero | Direction cosine |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `max_queue` | 37.386 | 28.653 | 30.5% worse | 0.300 |
| `nearest_buoy` | 26.273 | 7.529 | 248.9% worse | 0.412 |
| `queue_rate_energy` | 64.945 | 90.630 | 28.3% better | 0.732 |
| `random_valid` | 37.012 | 38.257 | 3.3% better | 0.260 |

The aggregate MSE gain is driven mainly by `queue_rate_energy`; `max_queue`
and `nearest_buoy` degrade. The signal is therefore learnable overall but not
consistently across policies.

Additional H=30 goal-conditioned RMSE:

- 3 buoys: 18.205 m; 5 buoys: 49.622 m;
- high quality: 29.789 m; mid quality: 51.041 m;
- short/medium/long episodes: 18.205 / 23.894 / 51.096 m.

These factors are heavily confounded in the fixed pilot. For example,
three-buoy cases are mostly short, high-quality trajectories, while mid-quality
cases are five-buoy horizon-limited trajectories. The differences cannot be
interpreted causally.

At H=30, 280/951 test targets are near zero. They contribute only 9.05% of
overall squared error, so aggregate error is not dominated by the
near-zero-direction convention. However, 241/254 `nearest_buoy` samples are
near zero, explaining why zero is exceptionally strong for that group.
Direction metrics are defined for 671/951 H=30 samples and exclude
near-zero pairs.

## Raw versus projected predictions

A 1 mm physical tolerance is used when deciding whether a prediction is
meaningfully outside the reachable disk. This is negligible relative to
10–300 m horizon scales but safely above float32 boundary round-off.

- Constant velocity lies on the physical boundary and now has zero projection
  activation for all H and both splits, rather than a false 10–35% activation
  rate caused by changes of only `10^-7` m.
- H=5/10/20/30 learned networks have zero goal-conditioned projection
  activation.
- H=1 goal-conditioned test activation averages 0.8245%; projection improves
  mean endpoint error by only about 0.0066 m.
- H=30 raw and projected predictions and metrics are identical.

The reported performance is not obtained by clipping implausible outputs.

## Scientific interpretation

The spatial target has an overall supervised signal at medium and long
horizons. H=30 is the validation-selected engineering interface and the
goal-conditioned model improves materially over its matched state-only
ablation in aggregate.

The evidence is deliberately qualified:

- validation H=30 RMSE is 29.25 m while test RMSE is 44.28 m;
- validation and test differ in target magnitude, near-zero share, policy
  composition, buoy count, quality, and episode length;
- only 80 episodes are available, and transition samples within an episode
  are strongly correlated;
- three seeds vary initialization, not dataset sampling;
- achieved-terminal goal is hindsight information;
- the dataset has no low-quality episodes;
- policy-specific performance is inconsistent;
- no closed-loop policy, return, or safety result exists.

Thus Round 04 supports **conditional engineering readiness** for a later
explicitly authorized, small, controlled Transformer-interface stage. It does
not support a claim that Waypoints are universally effective or deployable.
